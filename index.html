MSc dissertation, 2025 to 2026, SCAPS-1D scripting

Improving the efficiency of a silicon/perovskite tandem solar cell through simulation

  • Individual project testing whether a non-toxic tin-based absorber, Cs2SnI6, could replace lead-based perovskites in a two-terminal tandem without losing efficiency.
  • Screened seven electron and hole transport layer combinations under realistic bulk and interface defects, finding an NiO/SnO2 pairing best at 32.74% efficiency against 26.87% for the organic PEDOT:PSS/C60 baseline.
  • Wrote a SCAPS-1D script that filters the solar spectrum through the top cell, feeds the transmitted light to the silicon subcell, and sweeps absorber thickness in 5 nm steps to find the current-matched geometry at 505 nm.

Research intern, September 2025 to June 2026

Photovoltaic device simulation, Teesside University

  • Year-long placement on a joint UK and India research programme with SRM Institute of Science and Technology and SR University, modelling silicon and perovskite tandem solar cells under two academic supervisors.
  • Owned the silicon subcell: built and optimised the three-layer p-i-n crystalline silicon model in SCAPS-1D while a colleague developed the perovskite stack, then integrated the two.
  • Ran layer-thickness sweeps to lift silicon subcell efficiency from a 25.96% literature baseline to 26.03%, then added bulk defects layer by layer to locate the real losses, finding the intrinsic and n-layers far more defect-sensitive than the p-layer, at 18.82% and 17.13%.
  • Showed that omitting defect physics inflates predicted tandem efficiency from a defensible 32.60 to 35.30% up to an unphysical 54.78%, the paper's central finding.

The tunnel junction is modelled as ideal. SCAPS-1D does not simulate tunnelling, so this is a stated limitation of the study.
